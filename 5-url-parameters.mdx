### **What Are URL Parameters?**

_Simply put, url parameters are data points you add inside of a URL._

_Lets say your URL was:_

<Note>
  [www.example.com/landingpage?utm\\\_source=Instagram&utm\\\_campaign=happy](http://www.example.com/landingpage?utm_source=Instagram&utm_campaign=happy)
</Note>

Everything before the "?" is your domain, and everything after is considered a URL parameter.

The data we pull from this example is:

- URL: <u>www.example.com/landingpage</u>
- URL Parameters:
  - UTM Source = Instagram
  - UTM Campaign = Happy

URL parameters are ESSENTIAL to track where people are coming from.

You can add URL parameters to your ads (videos below) or custom URL parameters if you wish to track things like email campaigns, YouTube Organic tracking, etc.

    # URL Parameters for Meta Ads

    Setting up URL parameters for your Meta ads is a straightforward process, but it’s the most critical step for accurate attribution. Here is how to get them installed and verified.

    ### 1. Adding Parameters to a Single Ad

    To get started, navigate to the **Ad** level in your Meta Ads Manager.

    1. Scroll down to the **Tracking** section.
    2. Locate the **URL parameters** field.
    3. Copy the specific parameter string provided in your dashboard and paste it directly into this box.

    ### 2. Bulk Editing Multiple Ads

    If you have dozens of ads to update, you don't need to do them one by one.

    1. Select all the ads you want to update from the Ads Manager list.
    2. Click **Edit**.
    3. Scroll down to the **Tracking** section and paste your URL parameters into the field.
    4. Hit **Publish**.

    <Warning>
      **Note on Publishing:** When you hit publish, Meta will momentarily pause your ads while they review the changes. This is a standard small change, so ads are typically back up and running within 5 to 15 minutes.
    </Warning>

    ### 3. Verifying Your Setup

    It's important to keep an eye on your tracking status to ensure everything is configured properly. There are two main ways to do this:

    ### In Meta Ads Manager

    I recommend customizing your columns in Ads Manager to include **URL parameters**. This allows you to visibly confirm at a glance which ads have the tracking code installed and which do not.

    ### In Your Attribution Dashboard

    Inside your tracking table, look for the **Tracking** column. This acts as a "root check" for your setup:

    - **Green Checkmark:** You’re good to go! The URL parameters are configured properly.
    - **Red Triangle:** There is an error in the string.
    - **Red X:** Parameters are missing entirely.

    <Tip>
      If you see anything other than a green checkmark, simply click the icon to see exactly what is missing or misconfigured so you can fix it quickly.
    </Tip>

    ```jsx
    utm_source=Meta+Ads&utm_medium={{adset.name}}&utm_campaign={{campaign.name}}&utm_content={{ad.name}}&campaign_id={{campaign.id}}&medium_id={{adset.id}}&content_id={{ad.id}}
    ```

    > IMPORTANT: we are backwards compatible with Hyros URL Parameters `h_ad_id={{ad.id}}`.  We recommend migrating to Cortana's url parameters due to data cleanliness and better organization. (Example, our url parameter template will include the name of the ad in the raw url, where as hyros will not)

    # Adding URL Parameters for Google Ads

    Adding URL parameters to your Google Ads account is quite simple. Whether you're updating your existing campaigns or starting a fresh one, here’s how to get your tracking set up.

    ### For Existing Campaigns

    If you already have Google ads running, follow these steps to bulk update your tracking:

    1. Navigate to **Campaigns** in your Google Ads dashboard.
    2. Click the **Settings** tab.
    3. Select all the campaigns you want to track.
    4. Click the **Edit** button and select **Change tracking templates**.
    5. Copy and paste the URL parameters below in your dashboard into the **New tracking template** field.
    6. Hit **Apply**.

    ### For New Campaigns

    If you’re creating a new campaign from scratch, you can set your tracking template during the setup process:

    1. Start your new campaign creation.
    2. Navigate to the **Campaign settings** section.
    3. Scroll to the bottom, click **More settings**, and then select **Campaign URL options**.
    4. Paste your tracking code into the **Tracking template** field.
    5. Click **Next** and continue with your campaign build—away you go!

    > Setting your tracking at the campaign level ensures that all ads within that campaign automatically inherit the correct URL parameters, saving you time and preventing manual errors.

    # Adding URL Parameters for TikTok Ads

    TikTok lets you add tracking parameters directly to each ad. You paste one template, and TikTok fills in the campaign, ad group, and ad details automatically every time someone clicks. Here is how to get it set up and confirmed.

    ### 1. Adding Parameters to a Single Ad

    URL parameters live at the **Ad** level in TikTok Ads Manager.

    1. Open the ad you want to track and find the **Destination** (or **Tracking**) section.
    2. Locate the **URL parameters** field.
    3. Copy the template from your dashboard and paste it directly into this box.
    4. Save the ad.

    <Tip>
      Paste the template **exactly** as it appears in your dashboard. The `__CAMPAIGN_ID__`, `__AID__`, and `__CID__` parts are placeholders that TikTok replaces with real values at the moment of the click. If you paste them into a normal browser, they will not fill in — that is expected.
    </Tip>

    ### 2. Bulk Editing Multiple Ads

    If you have several ads to update, you do not need to edit them one at a time.

    1. Select all the ads you want to track from the ad list.
    2. Choose **Edit** (bulk edit).
    3. Paste your URL parameters into the **URL parameters** field.
    4. Save your changes.

    ### 3. Verifying Your Setup

    Inside your tracking table, look for the **URL Params** column, exactly like Meta, Google, and Bing:

    - **Green Checkmark:** Configured correctly.
    - **Red Triangle:** There is an error in the string.
    - **Red X:** Parameters are missing.
    - **Grey Dash ("Not applicable"):** This ad type cannot have URL parameters — see the Smart+ note below. This is not an error, and nothing needs fixing.

    <Tip>
      Click the icon on any ad to see exactly what is missing or misconfigured, and to copy the correct template.
    </Tip>

    ## URL Template: TikTok Ads

    <Note>
      ```text wrap
      utm_source=TikTok+Ads&utm_medium=__AID_NAME__&utm_campaign=__CAMPAIGN_NAME__&utm_content=__CID_NAME__&campaign_id=__CAMPAIGN_ID__&medium_id=__AID__&content_id=__CID__
      ```
    </Note>

    <Warning>
      **TikTok's placeholder names are the opposite of what you would expect.** `__AID__` is the **ad group** ID (not the ad), and `__CID__` is the **ad** ID. The template above already maps them the right way, so paste it as-is and do not rearrange the pieces.
    </Warning>

    <Warning>
      **Do not add `ttclid` yourself.** TikTok appends its own click identifier (`ttclid`) to every ad click automatically. It is how Cortana recognises a paid TikTok click, and it is **required for TikTok Events (CAPI)** to send conversions back to TikTok. Adding it by hand can break the click.
    </Warning>

    ### A note on Smart+ campaigns

    TikTok's **Smart+** campaigns generate their creative and destination automatically, and TikTok does **not** share a landing page URL for them through the API. Because there is no URL to read, there are no parameters to check — so these ads show a grey **"Not applicable"** dash in the URL Params column instead of a red error.

    <Note>
      This is expected behaviour, not a problem to fix. Standard (non‑Smart+) TikTok campaigns support the full template above and will show a green checkmark once tagged correctly.
    </Note>

    # Adding URL Parameters for Microsoft (Bing) Ads

    Microsoft Advertising uses **tracking templates** — the same idea as Google Ads. You paste one template and every ad underneath it inherits the tracking automatically. Microsoft applies templates in a cascade (**account → campaign → ad group → ad**), so setting it once at the account level covers everything.

    ### For Existing Campaigns

    If you already have Microsoft ads running, set the template once at the account level:

    1. In **Microsoft Advertising**, open **Settings**.
    2. Go to **Account level options** (or **Tracking template** under URL options).
    3. Paste the template below in your dashboard into the **Tracking template** field.
    4. Click **Save**.

    ### For New Campaigns

    You can also set the tracking template while building a campaign:

    1. Start your new campaign.
    2. In the campaign settings, open **Campaign URL options**.
    3. Paste your tracking template into the **Tracking template** field.
    4. Continue building your campaign.

    > Setting the template at the account or campaign level means every ad inside inherits the correct parameters automatically — no need to tag ads one by one.

    ### Verifying Your Setup

    Inside your tracking table, look for the **URL Params** column, exactly like Meta and Google:

    - **Green Checkmark:** Configured correctly.
    - **Red Triangle:** There's an error in the string.
    - **Red X:** Parameters are missing.

    <Tip>
      Click the icon on any ad to see exactly what's missing or misconfigured, and to copy the correct template for that campaign type.
    </Tip>

    ## URL Template: Search, Audience & Dynamic Search Ads

    <Note>
      ```text wrap
      {lpurl}?utm_source=Bing+Ads&campaign_id={CampaignId}&medium_id={AdGroupId}&ad_id={AdId}&utm_term={keyword}
      ```
    </Note>

    ## URL Template: Shopping & Performance Max

    Microsoft Shopping and Performance Max campaigns have **no ad-level or asset-group macros**, so campaign is the deepest level available — `campaign_id` alone is the correct, complete tag for these campaign types.

    <Note>
      ```text wrap
      {lpurl}?utm_source=Bing+Ads&campaign_id={CampaignId}
      ```

    <Warning>
      Keep **auto-tagging on** in Microsoft Advertising. The `msclkid` it adds is Cortana's fallback when a click carries no other identifiers, and it is **required for Microsoft (Bing) CAPI** to send conversions back.
    </Warning>

    > **Coming from Hyros?** Leave your existing Microsoft Ads templates exactly as they are — Cortana reads the Hyros Bing parameters (`h_campaign_id`, `bng_id`, `h_ad_id`) natively, so your attribution keeps working without touching a thing. If you'd like cleaner data going forward, you can migrate to the Cortana templates above, but it is not required.

    # Adding URL Parameters for LinkedIn Ads

    LinkedIn gives you one thing for free and one thing you have to set up.

    **Free:** LinkedIn adds its own click identifier, `li_fat_id`, to every ad click. Cortana reads it, so anyone arriving from a LinkedIn ad is credited to **LinkedIn Ads** with no setup at all. You never add it and you must never strip it.

    **What you set up:** LinkedIn's **dynamic URL tracking parameters**. These are placeholders LinkedIn fills in at click time, the same idea as Meta's `{{ad.name}}` or Google's `{campaignid}`. They are what tell Cortana *which* campaign, ad set, and ad produced the click. Without them, every LinkedIn sale arrives under one undifferentiated **LinkedIn Ads** line and you cannot compare one creative against another.

    <Warning>
      **Read the next section before you copy any template.** LinkedIn is part-way through renaming its ad hierarchy, and the same token means two different things depending on which naming your account has. Copying the wrong one is the single most damaging mistake you can make here.
    </Warning>

    ## Step 1: Check which naming your account uses

    LinkedIn is renaming its hierarchy from **Account → Campaign Group → Campaign → Creative** to the industry-standard **Account → Campaign → Ad Set → Ad**. LinkedIn began this in October 2025, describes it as gradual, and has published no completion date, so as of now both versions are still in the wild. Two ad accounts belonging to the same company can be on different versions at the same time.

    Open **Campaign Manager** and look at what your levels are called.

    | What you see | Which template to use |
    | --- | --- |
    | **Campaign groups** | Template A below |
    | **Ad sets** | Template B below |

    <Warning>
      **`{{CAMPAIGN_ID}}` appears in both naming schemes and means a different level in each.** Under the old naming it is an ad set. Under the new naming it is a campaign. This is why you have to check first rather than pick the template that looks right.
    </Warning>

    ## URL Template A: accounts that still show "Campaign groups"

    <Note>
      ```text wrap
      utm_source=LinkedIn&utm_campaign={{CAMPAIGN_GROUP_NAME}}&utm_medium={{CAMPAIGN_NAME}}&utm_content={{CREATIVE_NAME}}&campaign_id={{CAMPAIGN_GROUP_ID}}&medium_id={{CAMPAIGN_ID}}&content_id={{CREATIVE_ID}}
      ```
    </Note>

    ## URL Template B: accounts that show "Ad sets"

    <Note>
      ```text wrap
      utm_source=LinkedIn&utm_campaign={{CAMPAIGN_NAME}}&utm_medium={{AD_SET_NAME}}&utm_content={{AD_NAME}}&campaign_id={{CAMPAIGN_ID}}&medium_id={{AD_SET_ID}}&content_id={{AD_ID}}
      ```
    </Note>

    Both templates say the same thing. They send your campaign into Cortana's campaign slot, your ad set into the medium slot, and your individual ad into the content slot, so the Attribution Table can break LinkedIn down the same way it breaks down Meta and Google.

    | Cortana parameter | Holds | Old LinkedIn name | New LinkedIn name |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | `campaign_id` · `utm_campaign` | Your campaign | Campaign group | Campaign |
    | `medium_id` · `utm_medium` | Your ad set | Campaign | Ad set |
    | `content_id` · `utm_content` | Your individual ad | Creative | Ad |

    <Note>
      `TODO: verify` before publishing. Both strings above are assembled from LinkedIn's own published token list and its rename table, and each piece is individually confirmed, but neither full string has yet been watched resolving in a live LinkedIn account. Test one migrated and one un-migrated account and confirm the landing URL carries real IDs rather than literal `{{...}}` text.
    </Note>

    ## Step 2: Where to put it

    Dynamic parameters are set at **account level** or **ad set level**, never on an individual ad. Setting them once at account level is the least work and covers everything underneath.

    - **Account level:** Campaign Manager → **Account settings** → **URL parameters**. LinkedIn describes this screen as *"Set account-level URL tracking parameters to apply for every ad set."*
    - **Ad set level:** inside the ad set creation or edit flow, in the optional **URL tracking parameters** step, which sits just after **Select an ad format**.

    Enter the template as a list of `key={{TOKEN}}` pairs. If your account gives you a single free-text box rather than one row per parameter, join them with `&` exactly as written above.

    <Tip>
      Editing your tracking parameters does **not** send your ads back for review, so it is safe to fix tagging on live ads without interrupting delivery.
    </Tip>

    ## Syntax rules that catch people out

    - **The braces go around the value only**, never the key and never the whole pair. `campaign_id={{CAMPAIGN_ID}}` is right. `{{campaign_id=CAMPAIGN_ID}}` is wrong.
    - **Two braces each side, and the token in capitals.** LinkedIn's rule is that the value must be in all capital letters with double curly brackets before and after it.
    - **Hyphens are not allowed in a parameter key.** `campaign-id` is rejected; `campaign_id` is correct. Every key in the templates above is already valid.
    - **Spaces in a value are written as `+`**, which is why `utm_source=LinkedIn+Ads` would be valid if you wanted a space in it.

    <Warning>
      **Remove any of these parameters that you have already hard-coded into your destination URL.** LinkedIn *adds* its parameters to whatever your URL already carries, and it does not deduplicate. If your landing page URL already ends in `?utm_source=linkedin` and you also set `utm_source` as a parameter, the served URL carries `utm_source` twice. LinkedIn's own guidance is that you must ensure your parameters are unique.
    </Warning>

    ## Limitations

    - **Conversation Ads and Message Ads do not support dynamic parameters.** These are the only two formats LinkedIn documents as unsupported. LinkedIn does not publish a list of formats that *are* supported, so treat anything else as untested rather than guaranteed.
    - **Lead Gen Form ads work differently.** A lead who fills in the form without ever reaching your site cannot carry anything in a landing page URL, because there is no page load. LinkedIn passes campaign detail on those through **hidden fields** on the form instead.
    - **Dynamic parameters cannot be set on an individual ad**, only at account or ad set level. Plain static parameters can be set at ad level.

    ## Verifying your setup

    <Warning>
      **The URL Params column does not check LinkedIn ads.** Cortana validates URL parameters for Meta, Google, Bing, and TikTok only, so do not read that column as a verdict on your LinkedIn tagging in either direction. `TODO: verify` what the column renders on a LinkedIn ad row.
    </Warning>

    Check it directly instead:

    1. Click one of your own LinkedIn ads.
    2. Look at the address bar on the page you land on. You should see real names and numbers, plus LinkedIn's own `li_fat_id` on the end. If you see the literal text `{{CAMPAIGN_ID}}` in the URL, the token is not resolving and you are almost certainly on the wrong template for your account's naming.
    3. Open **Ad Tracking** in Cortana and confirm the visit appears under **LinkedIn Ads** with your campaign and ad names filled in rather than blank.

    <Note>
      **If you tag nothing at all, nothing breaks.** LinkedIn spend and LinkedIn's own reported conversions still sync, and clicks are still credited to **LinkedIn Ads** through `li_fat_id`. What you lose is the breakdown by campaign, ad set, and ad. An untagged ad shows up unattributed at those levels rather than being counted against the wrong campaign.
    </Note>

    <Warning>
      **Never strip `li_fat_id`, and never add it by hand.** LinkedIn appends it itself. It is the one part of LinkedIn attribution that works whether or not you tag anything. Redirect chains and link shorteners are where it usually gets lost, so if you send LinkedIn traffic through either, confirm the parameters survive to the final page.
    </Warning>

    <Note>
      Your Cortana tracking pixel must be on the landing page for any of this to work, tagged or not. See [**2) Setup Pixel**](/2-setup-pixel).
    </Note>

    # Setting Up Whop Ads

    Whop Ads lets you run **Meta (Facebook and Instagram) ads through Whop's ad manager**. Because the ads are built and served inside Whop, the setup is different from Meta, Google, TikTok, and Bing in two important ways:

    - You connect Whop with an **API key**, not a login.
    - Whop adds its own tracking parameters to every ad link **automatically**. There is no template for you to paste.

    Your only job is to connect the account and make sure the [Cortana pixel](/2-setup-pixel) is on the page your ads point to. Cortana handles the rest.

    ### 1. Connecting Whop Ads

    You connect Whop Ads with your **Company ID** and a **Company API key**.

    1. In Cortana, go to **Settings** and open the **Integrations** tab.
    2. Find the **Whop Ads** card and click **Connect**.
    3. Enter your **Company ID**. You will find this in your Whop dashboard URL. For example, in `dash.whop.com/biz_abc123xyz`, your Company ID is `biz_abc123xyz`.
    4. Enter your **API key**. You create or edit an API key in your Whop dashboard, using the permissions listed below.
    5. Choose how much past spend to **backfill**, then click **Connect**.

    <Note>
      Already using Whop for conversion tracking? Use the **same** API key and simply add the ad permissions to it. One key covers both the ads side and the conversions side.
    </Note>

    **Required permissions on your Whop API key:**

    | Permission | What it is for |
    | --- | --- |
    | `ad_campaign:read` | Read your campaigns, ad groups, and ads |
    | `ad_campaign:stats:read` | Read spend and performance stats |
    | `event:create` | Send conversions back to Whop (see step 5) |
    | `ad_campaign:update` | Optional. Only needed to pause campaigns or edit budgets from inside Cortana |

    ### 2. What Gets Synced

    Once connected, Cortana pulls your Whop ad account into the same views as your other ad platforms:

    - Your **campaigns, ad groups, and ads**, with their status and budgets.
    - **Daily spend and performance** (impressions, clicks, reach, results, and ROAS).

    Whop then appears as its own source, **Whop Ads**, in your attribution table, right next to Meta, Google, TikTok, and Bing. Syncing runs automatically every hour.

    <Note>
      Whop does not share ad creative images through its API, so image previews will not appear for Whop ads. All of your spend and performance numbers still sync normally.
    </Note>

    ### 3. Tracking Parameters (Added Automatically)

    This is the key difference from the other platforms. **You do not paste a URL template for Whop Ads.** Whop adds its own tracking parameters to the ad link at the moment someone clicks, and the Cortana pixel reads them on your landing page.

    Whop adds these parameters:

    | Parameter | What it identifies |
    | --- | --- |
    | `waid` | The Whop **ad** |
    | `wasid` | The Whop **ad group** (ad set) |
    | `wacid` | The Whop **campaign** |
    | `utm_whop` | Marks the click as Whop traffic |

    When someone clicks a Whop ad and lands on your page, the link looks like the example below. Whop fills in the real IDs for you, so this is only to show you what to expect:

    <Note>
      ```text wrap
      yourlandingpage.com/vsl?utm_whop=true&waid=WHOP_AD_ID&wasid=WHOP_AD_GROUP_ID&wacid=WHOP_CAMPAIGN_ID
      ```
    </Note>

    Because these are Meta ads underneath, the link also carries the normal Meta identifiers. Cortana treats **Whop as the owner** of the click, so the traffic groups cleanly under **Whop Ads** instead of splitting off under Meta.

    <Warning>
      **Do not add `waid`, `wasid`, or `wacid` yourself.** Whop appends them automatically, and adding them by hand can break the link. Your only responsibility is to make sure the **Cortana pixel is installed** on the page your Whop ads point to. Without the pixel, there is nothing to read these parameters.
    </Warning>

    ### 4. Verifying Your Setup

    1. On the **Whop Ads** settings page, confirm the account shows as **Connected** with a recent **Last synced** time.
    2. Open your **attribution table** and look for the **Whop Ads** source. Your campaigns, spend, and results should appear there.

    <Tip>
      If your spend has not shown up yet, give it up to an hour for the first sync, or click **Refresh** on the Whop Ads settings page.
    </Tip>

    ### 5. Sending Conversions Back to Whop (CAPI)

    Whop Ads can also send your conversions **back to Whop**, the same idea as Meta CAPI. When a tracked visitor converts, Cortana tells Whop, so Whop's ad algorithm learns who your real buyers are and finds more people like them.

    You set this up on the [AI Ad Optimization](/6-ai-ad-optimization) page:

    1. Find the **Whop Conversions API** card and connect it. It reuses the same API key, so there is no pixel or token to paste.
    2. Turn the toggle **on** so the card shows **Active**.
    3. Click **Manage** to map your conversion events to Whop's event types. For example, a booked call maps to **Schedule**, and a sign up maps to **Complete Registration**. Anything without a direct match is sent as a **Custom** event.
    4. Use **Send test event** to confirm Whop is accepting your events.

    <Note>
      Conversions are matched to Whop on the buyer's real details (email, phone, and name), and each one is sent only once, so re-syncing never double counts. This requires the `event:create` permission on your API key.
    </Note>

    ## Overview

    Cortana's **Attribution Builder** lets you tag any organic traffic source — YouTube, Instagram, email, or anything else — so you can see exactly which content drives opt-ins, calls, and purchases inside your attribution table.

    You don't _need_ Cortana to build UTM links, but the builder makes it dead simple and keeps your naming consistent.

    <Note>
      The page you're sending traffic to **must have the Cortana pixel installed**. Without it, page views and visitor tracking won't work — none of this data will show up.
    </Note>

    ## Accessing the Attribution Builder

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open URL Builder">
        Navigate to **Ad Tracking** → **URL Builder**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Switch to the Attribution tab">
        Click the **Attribution Builder** tab at the top of the URL Builder page.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    ## Understanding UTM Dimensions

    Think of UTMs like **nested folders** that organize where your traffic came from. Each layer gets more specific:

    | Dimension | What it represents | Example |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | **Source** | The platform | `YouTube Organic`, `Instagram Organic`, `Email` |
    | **Campaign** | The channel or project | `mateis-channel`, `weekly-newsletter` |
    | **Medium** | The specific piece of content | `awesome-video-1` |
    | **Ad** | The exact placement or link | `link-in-bio`, `pinned-comment` |

    <Tip>
      There's no single "perfect" structure — just think top-down. What's the broadest category? What's underneath it? Keep nesting until you've captured enough detail to be useful.
    </Tip>

    ## Building a Tracking URL

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Enter your destination URL">
        Paste the page URL you're sending traffic to (e.g. `mylandingpage.com/vsl`). Optionally give it a title for your own reference.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Add UTM dimensions">
        Fill in the dimensions from top to bottom:

        - **Source** → `YouTube Organic`
        - **Campaign** → `mateis-channel`
        - **Medium** → `awesome-video-1`
        - **Ad** → `awesome-video-1-link-in-bio`

        Click **Create** after entering each value.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Copy the generated URL">
        The builder appends query parameters to your base URL automatically:

        ```text
        mylandingpage.com/vsl?utm_source=YouTube+Organic&utm_medium=awesome-video-1&utm_campaign=mateis-channel&utm_content=awesome-video-1-link-in-bio
        ```

        Everything after the `?` is tracking data — the visitor still sees the same page.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Place the link">
        Paste the generated URL wherever the traffic will come from (link in bio, video description, email CTA, etc.).

    ## How the Data Shows Up

    Once someone clicks your tagged link, Cortana tracks their full journey. Inside the **Attribution Table** you can:

    - Filter by **source** (e.g. `YouTube Organic`) to see all traffic from that platform.
    - Drill into **campaign** to compare channels or projects.
    - Go deeper into **medium** and **ad** to see which specific video or link placement drove the conversion.

    If you left a dimension empty, the table will display **"No campaign provided"** (or the equivalent for that level) — which is totally fine if you don't need that layer of detail.

    ## Keeping It Simple (Email Example)

    Not every source needs four layers. For email, if you just want to know "this came from email" without breaking it down further, you can set **all four dimensions** to `email`:

    | Dimension | Value |
    | --- | --- |
    | Source | `email` |
    | Campaign | `email` |
    | Medium | `email` |
    | Ad | `email` |

    This keeps your attribution table clean without creating unnecessary granularity.

    <Info>
      If you only fill in **Source** and leave the rest blank, the attribution table will show the source correctly but display "no campaign provided" when you try to drill down. Fill all four with the same value if you want a clean look without extra detail.
    </Info>
