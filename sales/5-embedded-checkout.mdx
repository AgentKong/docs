An embedded checkout lets you sell directly on your own page. You build it once, paste a single snippet where the checkout should appear, and Cortana handles attribution, rep credit, and commissions with no extra tracking code.

## Open Embedded Checkout

Go to **Payment Links**, then the **Embedded Checkout** sub-tab. Existing checkouts are listed with their offer, badges, and live stats.

## Create your checkout

Click **New Embedded Checkout** to open the builder. It walks three steps (**The offer**, **On the page**, then **Done**) with a live preview on the right. For Stripe checkouts the preview runs in Stripe test mode so nothing is charged while you build.

The first choice is the **Payment platform**: **Stripe**, **Whop**, or **Fanbasis**. This decides which checkout renders on your page and cannot be changed after the checkout is saved.

- **Stripe**: the full builder below, with prices, order bumps, trials, and discounts.
- **Whop**: pick your **Whop company**, then the **Plan** the buyer purchases. Whop renders its own checkout inside your page when the snippet loads.
- **Fanbasis**: pick your **Fanbasis account**, then the **Product**. Fanbasis renders its checkout inside your page in a secure frame, with a fallback link if the frame cannot load.

Order bumps, free trials, and discounts are Stripe features; a Whop or Fanbasis checkout sells the one plan or product you picked.

### Configure the offer (Stripe)

<Frame caption="Configuring the offer: choose On-page (1) so the form renders in your page, pick the price (2), and turn on an order bump (3) to offer an add-on at checkout.">
  <img src="/images/sales-dashboard/embedded-checkout/02-setup.jpg" alt="Embedded checkout builder with On-page selected, a recurring price chosen, and an order bump toggled on with an add-on price, beside a live preview of the on-page checkout" />
</Frame>

1. **How does it show up?** Choose **On-page** so the checkout form renders directly in your page and buyers never leave. **Popup** shows a button that opens it in a modal, and **Stripe page** sends them to a Stripe-hosted page. With On-page selected, the live preview shows the exact form buyers will see.
2. **What are they buying?** Pick one or more prices. A recurring price makes it a **Subscription** and unlocks a **Free trial (days)** field and the **After the first billing period** dropdown (see Intro offer below). If the product does not exist yet, **New product** creates it on the spot.
3. **Order bump.** Turn this on to offer an add-on the buyer can tick at checkout, the low-ticket upsell classic. Pick the add-on price. You can also apply a **Discount** here.

Name the checkout (just for you, buyers never see it), pick the **Stripe account** that gets paid, then click **Create checkout**.

## Intro offer: switch price after the first billing period

Sell a subscription at one price for its first billing period, then move the buyer to a different price automatically. The classic shape is an intro month followed by a quarterly plan, for example $297 for the first month, then $891 every 3 months. Because the buyer is on an active subscription from day one, the sale counts as recurring revenue immediately in Stripe. A free trial does not: it reports $0 until it converts.

{/* TODO: screenshot images/sales-dashboard/embedded-checkout/03-intro-offer.jpg, the offer step with the After the first billing period dropdown open and the live preview showing the disclosure line above the pay button */}

1. In **What are they buying?**, pick exactly **one recurring price**. This is the intro price and it is what the buyer pays at checkout.
2. Open **After the first billing period** and choose **Switch to** the price the subscription should move to. The list shows the other recurring prices on the same Stripe account. The default, **Keep billing the same price**, is a normal subscription.
3. Check the live preview. Stripe shows the intro price, and directly above the pay button Cortana adds one line spelling out the full offer, for example "$297.00 for your first month, then $891.00 every 3 months." The buyer always sees both prices before paying.
4. Optional but recommended: on the **On the page** step, require custom terms with the same wording, so the buyer also ticks a box on the full offer. See Consent and terms below.

Picking a switch price clears the **Free trial (days)** field, and typing a trial clears the switch: the intro price is the first billing period, so the two cannot be combined.

## Consent and terms (chargeback protection)

If your offer has conditions, a money-back guarantee with rules, or an attendance requirement, collect the buyer's explicit agreement at checkout. Cortana shows your terms as a checkbox the buyer **must** tick before they can pay. Leave it unchecked and press pay, and the checkout blocks with a please-check-consent message, so no one buys without agreeing.

<Frame caption="The buyer must tick your terms (1) before the pay button (2) will submit. The checkout blocks the payment until they do.">
  <img src="/images/sales-dashboard/embedded-checkout/04-consent.jpg" alt="On-page checkout with a required terms-of-service checkbox listing the money-back guarantee conditions above the Pay and start trial button" />
</Frame>

Edit this wording with the **Consents** action on the checkout card. On a Stripe checkout, Stripe itself enforces and records the checkbox with the payment. On a Whop or Fanbasis checkout, Cortana shows your terms as its own required checkbox **before** the checkout is even created: nothing payable exists until the buyer ticks it, and the acceptance rides with the payment. Either way, it is exactly the evidence you need to win a chargeback: proof the customer saw and agreed to your terms at the moment of purchase.

## The consent certificate

Every accepted consent automatically produces a stamped **Certificate of Consent**: a one-page PDF that reads as proof, not as a database export. It is the document you attach when a buyer disputes a charge and claims they never agreed to your terms.

<Frame caption="A Certificate of Consent. Buyer, IP, session id, and verification code are blurred here; the real PDF shows them in full.">
  <img src="/images/sales-dashboard/embedded-checkout/06-consent-certificate.jpg" alt="Certificate of Consent PDF with a Proof of Consent stamp, a table of buyer, IP address at checkout, device, amount paid, payment platform, accepted-at timestamp, and checkout session, the verbatim terms in a box, and a verification code at the bottom" />
</Frame>

### What the certificate records

| Field | What it is | Why it matters in a dispute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Buyer** | Name and email the buyer entered at checkout | Ties the acceptance to the cardholder |
| **IP address at checkout** | The address of the browser that opened the checkout | Places the buyer at the purchase; this is their browser, not the card processor's network |
| **Device / user agent** | The full browser string | Matches the device the buyer normally uses |
| **Amount paid** and **Payment platform** | The charge and where it ran (Stripe, Whop, or Fanbasis) | Links the consent to the exact transaction being disputed |
| **Accepted at** | UTC timestamp of the tick | Proves consent came before the payment, not after |
| **Checkout session** | The processor's session id | Lets the processor cross-check its own record |
| **Terms accepted (verbatim)** | Your terms wording exactly as the buyer saw it | The core evidence: what they agreed to, word for word |
| **Verification code** | A code derived from the immutable consent record | Proves the PDF has not been edited |

### How it is built

1. **Capture at the moment of purchase.** When the buyer ticks the box and pays, Cortana stores an immutable record of consent: the buyer, IP, device, amount, platform, timestamp, session id, and the exact terms text shown. The record is never edited after that. On Stripe, the checkbox itself is collected and attested by Stripe's own consent mechanism; Cortana keeps the surrounding evidence.
2. **Render the certificate.** A background job turns the record into the stamped PDF within moments of the purchase. Nothing is retyped: the fields are reproduced from the record unaltered, and the terms box is the wording the buyer saw, verbatim.
3. **Sign it with a verification code.** The code at the bottom is computed from the consent record. Regenerating the certificate from the same record always produces the same code, so anyone who questions the document can have it regenerated and compare. A mismatch means the document was altered.
4. **Generate on demand if needed.** If you click download before the background job has finished, Cortana generates the certificate on the spot from the same record. You never wait and you never get a blank.

### Where to find it

<Frame caption="The Checkout Consent Accepted event in a contact's Customer Journey, with the Download certificate button. Contact details are blurred here.">
  <img src="/images/sales-dashboard/embedded-checkout/07-journey-consent-event.jpg" alt="Contact profile with the Customer Journey panel showing a green Checkout Consent Accepted event, Terms accepted at checkout paid 297 dollars, and a Download certificate button, between a Page View and two Stripe conversion events" />
</Frame>

1. **The customer journey timeline.** Open the contact and look for the green **Checkout Consent Accepted** event. It sits in sequence with the page view that led to the checkout and the Stripe payment that followed, so the whole story is on one screen. Click **Download certificate** to get the PDF.
2. **The Consents dialog.** Click **Consents** on the checkout card, open a buyer's signed agreement, and click **Download certificate** next to **Print dispute packet**.

### Using it in a chargeback

When a dispute lands, open the buyer's journey, download the certificate, and upload it as your evidence along with the **dispute packet** from the Consents dialog. The processor sees the buyer's identity, IP, device, timestamp, and the verbatim terms in one stamped document, plus the session id to match against its own record. Pair it with the journey itself if the dispute claims the buyer never visited: the page views, the consent, and the payment are timestamped in order.

<Tip>
  The certificate is only as strong as your terms. Write the conditions the buyer is most likely to dispute later, such as a guarantee tied to the first payment, attendance requirements, or what counts as proof of an issue, directly into the consent text. Whatever is in that box is what the certificate proves they agreed to.
</Tip>

## Put it on your page

When you finish the builder (the **Done** step), or click **Snippet** on any checkout card, you get the embed code and where to paste it.

<Frame caption="Copy the snippet (2), choose the tracking pixel for the site, and follow the three steps (3) to paste it into your page builder.">
  <img src="/images/sales-dashboard/embedded-checkout/05-snippet.jpg" alt="Put it on your page dialog with a tracking-pixel selector, the embed snippet code, a Copy snippet button, and three steps to paste it into a page builder" />
</Frame>

1. Choose the **tracking pixel** for the site the checkout will live on (this is what carries attribution).
2. **Copy snippet** and paste it into a custom HTML or embed block in your page builder: GHL, Webflow, WordPress, or anything that takes custom HTML, exactly where the checkout should sit.
3. Publish. Sales, contacts, and attribution flow in automatically.

Each checkout card also has **Preview** (see what buyers see), **Consents** (edit the terms), and the **power** icon (turn it off, which stops it selling immediately).

<Tip>
  The snippet lives on **your** page. Cortana does not host a testimonials wrapper around it. For a high-ticket send, save that page URL as a tracking link and have the rep send the tracking link instead of a payment link. See [Send a sales page](/sales/send-a-sales-page).
</Tip>

## Read the stats

Each checkout shows its own funnel: **Viewed**, **Clicked**, **Opened**, **Emails**, **Completed**, **Conv. %**, and **Revenue**, so you can see how the page is converting.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Badges on a checkout">
    - **On-page** (or popup, or hosted): how the checkout displays.
    - **Subscription** or one-time: derived from the prices.
    - **30-day trial** or similar: shown when a free trial is set.
    - **Intro offer**: the subscription switches to another price after its first billing period.
    - **custom terms required**: the checkout collects terms-of-service acceptance. On Stripe this needs a Terms of Service URL on the connected Stripe account; on Whop and Fanbasis, Cortana shows the checkbox itself, so nothing extra is needed.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How an intro offer bills and switches">
    At checkout the buyer starts a normal subscription on the intro price and pays its first period right away. Cortana then attaches a Stripe subscription schedule with two phases: the intro price until the end of that first period, then the switch price. From the switch onward the subscription simply renews on the switch price, for as long as the buyer stays. There is no gap and no second checkout.

    Rules the builder enforces when you save:
    - The main items must contain exactly one recurring price (the intro price). One-time prices can sit beside it.
    - The switch price must be a recurring price on the same Stripe account, in the same currency as the intro price, and not the intro price itself.
    - Order bumps must be one-time prices.
    - A free trial and an intro offer cannot be combined.

    Discounts and promo codes apply to the intro period. The buyer sees the intro price in Stripe's checkout summary plus the full-offer line above the pay button. In Stripe, the subscription shows the upcoming price change. Cancelling the subscription cancels the scheduled switch too. If you change the price directly in Stripe, Cortana leaves your change alone. If a switch ever fails to attach, Cortana retries every hour and flags it, so a buyer is never left on the intro price by accident.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Session expiry, abandoned emails, and auto-refresh">
    Each Stripe checkout session stays payable for 1 hour after it opens on the page. The widget watches for returning visitors: if someone comes back to a checkout that has gone stale (for example a tab left open, or the browser Back button), it silently swaps in a fresh session, so they never meet a dead payment form.

    When a session expires without a payment, Stripe tells Cortana whether the visitor had typed an email into the checkout. When that email is reported, Cortana counts it under **Emails** and creates or updates the matching contact, linked to that visitor's tracked session, so they appear identified in Page Views and Contacts with the source "abandoned checkout". Stripe also issues a recovery link for the abandoned session, which Cortana stores with the capture for future win-back tooling.

    Closing the checkout popup or leaving the page reports the abandon right away. Otherwise it is reported when the 1-hour session expires. A visitor who merely scrolls past the checkout without typing anything is never captured, and a visitor who pays is never counted as an abandon.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why the snippet needs a tracking pixel">
    The snippet is built from your business's tracking pixel so it can attribute the sale. If your business has no pixel yet, create one under Ad Tracking, then Tracking Pixels, then build the checkout.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Building checkouts with the API or an AI tool">
    Developers and AI page builders can create these same checkouts through the Cortana API and MCP. See the Dev Docs, Add a Cortana checkout with AI.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Is the checkout live as soon as I create it?">
    Yes. For Stripe, check whether it is on a live or test Stripe account before you share the page. Whop and Fanbasis checkouts sell against your live connected account from the start.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why is there no live preview for my Whop or Fanbasis checkout?">
    Whop and Fanbasis render their own checkout inside your page when the snippet loads, so there is nothing Cortana can safely mount inside the builder. The preview pane explains this; paste the snippet on a draft page to see the real thing.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The Snippet button gave me nothing to paste.">
    The business has no tracking pixel yet, so the snippet cannot be built. Create a pixel under Ad Tracking, then Tracking Pixels, and try again.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do I charge one price for the first month and a different price after?">
    Build a subscription with the intro price as the only recurring main item, then set **After the first billing period** to the price you want next. The buyer pays the intro price at checkout and moves to the other price automatically when the first period ends.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why can't I set a free trial on my intro offer?">
    The intro price is the first billing period, so there is nothing to trial. Choose one: a free trial (nothing charged today) or an intro offer (the intro price charged today, then the switch). Picking one clears the other.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does the buyer see the price they will switch to?">
    Yes. Stripe's checkout shows the intro price, and one line above the pay button states the full offer, for example "$297.00 for your first month, then $891.00 every 3 months." For extra protection, require custom terms with the same wording so the buyer ticks a box on it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do I stop a checkout without deleting it?">
    Use the power icon to turn it off. It stops rendering immediately, and its history and stats are kept so you can turn it back on later.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A visitor typed their email into the checkout but never paid. Where do I see them?">
    When Stripe reports that email on the abandoned session, the checkout's **Emails** count goes up and the person shows up in Contacts with the source "abandoned checkout", linked to their tracked visit. Closing the popup or leaving the page reports it within seconds; a session that just sits there reports when it expires, about an hour after it opened.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why did a buyer see a session expired message?">
    A checkout session stays payable for 1 hour. The widget refreshes a stale checkout automatically when the visitor comes back to the tab or moves their cursor over it, so this is rare: it can still happen if someone leaves the form open and idle for over an hour and then pays without any signal the widget can see. They can reload the page and pay normally, and no charge was made.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does an abandoned email count the same visitor twice?">
    Counts are per checkout session. If the same visitor opens the checkout on two occasions and types their email both times, Emails counts two, but Contacts still holds a single person: every capture resolves to the same contact by email.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A buyer filed a chargeback and says they never agreed to my terms. What do I send?">
    Open their contact, find the green **Checkout Consent Accepted** event in the journey, and click **Download certificate**. Upload the PDF as your dispute evidence. It records who bought, their IP and device, the timestamp, the amount, the checkout session id, and your terms word for word, with a verification code that proves the document is unaltered. For the fuller bundle, use **Print dispute packet** in the Consents dialog.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can a certificate be edited or faked?">
    Not without detection. The verification code is derived from Cortana's immutable record of the consent. Regenerating the certificate from that record reproduces the same code, so an edited PDF will not match. If a processor or buyer questions it, regenerate and compare.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do reps send this page to one contact?">
    Do not send a payment link. Save the page URL as a tracking link, then send that tracking link for the selected contact. Same motion as the payment URL builder. See [Send a sales page](/sales/send-a-sales-page).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
